\chapter{Introduction}

Modern supply chains, retail systems, and energy networks increasingly depend on
their ability to shape demand rather than merely respond to it. Traditional
approaches to demand shaping—such as static discounts, broad loyalty programs,
or heuristic promotional rules—fail to account for the dynamic and behavioral
nature of consumer decision-making. As a result, organizations often deploy
incentives inefficiently, overspend on promotions, and miss opportunities to
influence long-term behavioral patterns.

The Behavioral Engine for Demand (BED) addresses these limitations by providing
a closed-loop, state-based, and optimization-driven framework for incentive
design. BED models individual behavior using latent variables that capture
recency, habit, and responsiveness, and it allocates incentives through a
Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) that respects budget and fairness
constraints. This thesis presents BED v3, the most complete and integrated
version of the framework to date, combining behavioral modeling, simulation,
prototype implementation, and experimental design into a unified research
architecture.

\section{Motivation}

Organizations increasingly recognize that consumer behavior is shaped by
patterns of engagement, habit formation, and responsiveness to incentives.
However, most operational systems treat incentives as static levers rather than
dynamic interventions. Three challenges motivate the development of BED:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Behavioral complexity}: Consumers exhibit recency effects,
    habit formation, and heterogeneous responsiveness that evolve over time.
    \item \textbf{Operational constraints}: Incentives must be allocated under
    strict budget, fairness, and exposure constraints.
    \item \textbf{Lack of closed-loop systems}: Most incentive programs do not
    adapt to real-time behavioral feedback or optimize long-term outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

These challenges create a gap between behavioral theory and operational
practice. BED aims to bridge this gap by integrating behavioral modeling with
optimization and experimentation.

\section{Research Questions}

This thesis investigates the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item How can individual behavior be represented as a Markovian state that
    captures recency, habit, and responsiveness?
    \item How do incentives influence state transitions, and how can these
    dynamics be modeled using a hazard-based framework?
    \item How can a CMDP be formulated to allocate incentives optimally under
    budget constraints?
    \item How can simulation be used to evaluate the performance of incentive
    policies before real-world deployment?
    \item What prototype architecture enables safe, scalable, and privacy-aware
    experimentation?
\end{itemize}

These questions guide the structure of the thesis and the development of BED v3.

\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes four primary contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Behavioral State Model}: A formal representation of recency,
    habit, and responsiveness as latent behavioral variables with interpretable
    update rules.
    \item \textbf{CMDP Formulation}: A constrained optimization framework for
    incentive allocation that balances behavioral impact with budgetary limits.
    \item \textbf{Simulation Environment}: A complete simulation engine that
    integrates behavioral dynamics, hazard modeling, and policy evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Prototype Implementation}: A QR-to-cloud prototype that
    demonstrates how BED can be deployed in real-world settings using
    serverless infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these components form a unified research framework for closed-loop
demand shaping.

\section{Thesis Structure}

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2} reviews the behavioral, operational, and
    reinforcement learning foundations of BED.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3} presents the system architecture and conceptual
    design of the Behavioral Engine for Demand.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 4} defines the behavioral state model and its update
    dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 5} formulates the incentive allocation problem as a
    Constrained Markov Decision Process.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 6} describes the simulation environment used to
    evaluate BED policies.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 7} documents the prototype architecture and
    implementation.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 8} outlines the experimental design and evaluation
    methodology.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 9} presents simulation results and behavioral
    trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 10} discusses implications, limitations, and
    extensions.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 11} concludes with contributions and future work.
\end{itemize}

This structure reflects the integrated nature of BED v3, combining theory,
simulation, and prototype implementation into a cohesive research contribution.

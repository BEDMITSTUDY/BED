\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Incentive systems are widely used to influence human behavior in domains such as retail, public health, transportation, and digital engagement. Yet most real-world incentive programs rely on static rules, fixed reward magnitudes, or heuristic timing strategies that do not account for behavioral dynamics, habit formation, heterogeneity, or operational constraints.

This manuscript introduces the \textbf{Behavioral Engine for Demand (BED)}, a unified behavioral and algorithmic framework that formulates incentive timing as a \textbf{Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)}. BED models individual behavior using hazard-based event probabilities, latent habit states, and responsiveness parameters that evolve over time. The CMDP incorporates budget limits, fairness constraints, and operational feasibility, enabling adaptive incentive allocation that is both behaviorally grounded and practically deployable.

The framework integrates three components: a behavioral state model, a CMDP policy engine, and a serverless prototype capable of real-time event logging and incentive delivery. A simulation environment evaluates policy performance across heterogeneous populations, allowing controlled experimentation with timing, magnitude, and targeting strategies.

Together, these elements establish BED as a rigorous, extensible platform for closed-loop demand shaping. The system provides a foundation for empirical testing, cross-domain experimentation, and future research across supply chain management, behavioral operations, and algorithmic decision-making.
